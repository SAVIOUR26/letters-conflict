There is substantial historical and archaeological evidence to work with.

Consider these examples:

Ancient Egypt, Nubia, and Kush: African civilizations that developed complex political systems, monumental architecture, religious traditions, and systems of writing. Aksum: An influential kingdom in present-day Ethiopia and Eritrea, connected to trade networks across Africa, Arabia, and the Mediterranean world. Its surviving monuments demonstrate sophisticated engineering and artistic traditions. 
The empires of Ghana, Mali, and Songhai: Major West African political and commercial powers. Timbuktu became a significant centre of scholarship, manuscript production, and trans-Saharan commerce.

The pattern of historical authorship. Who documented African societies? Who interpreted their monuments, manuscripts, political systems, and oral histories? Whose interpretations became authoritative?

The pattern of education. What histories were taught during colonial rule, and what continued into post-independence school systems? Why might a student know more about distant European rulers than the intellectual traditions of a nearby African civilization?

The pattern of material loss. How did warfare, environmental change, enslavement, conquest, looting, and the displacement of cultural objects affect historical continuity?
