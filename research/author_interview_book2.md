# Author interview — Book Two, *Somewhere in Africa*

Answer in your own words; rough notes and voice-note transcripts are perfect. These answers become the **Witnessed** scenes and the Introduction ("Somewhere, Every Day"). Nothing in the book will be presented as witnessed unless it comes from here. You don't need to name real places or people; describe what you saw.

## The Introduction
1. When did you first notice that the same "sentences" (the same patterns of behaviour) were being written around you, not just in the past?
2. Was there one moment that made you stop blaming only history or only "them"?

## Scenes you have seen or lived (one or two lines each is enough)
3. **Small exceptions:** a time a rule bent for someone who "knew someone", or a queue that didn't count.
4. **A signature with a price:** a time you or someone near you was asked to pay for something that should have been free. How did it feel, and what was decided?
5. **Education:** a family's sacrifice for school fees, and what happened after graduation. Your own experience, or someone close to you?
6. **Work and the street:** a graduate waiting at home; the kifeesi; a robbery or a young person you saw drift into the street.
7. **Health:** a day of waiting at a hospital or health centre.
8. **Roads and projects:** a road, bridge or project you watched being built, rebuilt or abandoned.
9. **Person and office:** an office, school, church or company that stopped working when one person was absent, or that became one person's property.
10. **Elections:** gifts, promises or pressure you have seen around a vote.
11. **Silence and exit:** a friend who left the country, or a time someone stayed silent to stay safe.
12. **The letter that held:** someone you know who refused to bend the rule when it would have paid, or an office that simply worked.

## Your view
13. As a founder (Thirdsan, KandaNews), where have you seen a system fail, and where have you made one work?
14. Who should this book be dedicated to?
