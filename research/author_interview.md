# Author interview — Najuna Saviour

Answer in your own words. Rough answers and voice-note transcripts are perfect — don’t polish them. These answers become the Introduction (“The Book I Started on Campus”), the classroom material in Chapter 10 and the closing of Chapter 11.

## The beginning
1. Where were you studying when you first wrote about Letters Conflict, and roughly what year was it?
2. What happened, or what did you see, that made you start writing? Describe one scene if you can.
3. Why “letters”? Where did that image come from: typing, the alphabet, scripture, something else?

## What you believed then
4. What did you believe back then about why Africa was “dark and far from civilisation”? Be honest, even if you no longer believe it.
5. What changed your mind, or complicated it? A book, a person, a place, something you found out?

## Your own history
6. How were you taught African history at school? What do you remember learning about Europe compared with Africa?
7. What did you learn about Buganda or other kingdoms at home or from elders? Is there any story you’d be comfortable including?
8. Is there a moment when you first felt pride in, or shame about, the African past?

## The present
9. Describe one ordinary thing you’ve seen in Uganda that made you think “the letters are in conflict.”
10. As a founder building institutions at Thirdsan, what have you learned first-hand about why systems fail or survive?
11. As a minister, how does your faith shape the way you think about responsibility, nations, or “meaning”?

## The reader
12. Picture one person reading Origins. Who are they, how old, where are they?
13. When they finish the book, what should they think, feel or do differently?
14. Who should the book be dedicated to?
